In modern pain neuroscience, few researchers have reshaped our understanding of chronic suffering more profoundly than Professor G. Lorimer Moseley and Dr. David Butler, authors of *Explain Pain*.

For anyone living with unexplained burning mouth pain, their foundational model, known clinically as the **"Cup of Tolerance"** (or overflowing bucket), provides the exact scientific framework needed to understand why symptoms suddenly erupt and how recovery actually occurs.

### The Cup of Tolerance Model

Moseley and Butler demonstrate that the human central nervous system operates like a protective reservoir. Throughout daily life, various inputs pour into this reservoir:
* **Biological and Mechanical Stressors:** Low-grade tissue inflammation, damaged dental margins, exposed microscopic root tubules, and dry oral mucosa.
* **Sensory Demands:** Repetitive mechanical tongue friction against sharp tooth edges, acidic dietary contact, and subgingival bacterial fermentation.
* **Systemic and Environmental Loads:** Poor sleep quality, hormonal fluctuations around menopause, and everyday psychological tension.

Under normal conditions, an individual's nervous system possesses ample buffer capacity to process these background signals quietly below conscious perception. 

However, when multiple inputs stack together over months or years, the total volume steadily rises. The moment cumulative load breaches the physical lip of the cup, the central nervous system sounds an emergency alarm: **chronic neuropathic pain**.

### The Illusion of the "Final Drop"

When a patient experiences a violent burning flare after a dental cleaning, a stressful week, or drinking a cup of coffee, they naturally assume that specific event "caused" their disease.

Moseley and Butler explain why this is an illusion. The coffee or dental scaling was simply the final milliliter that caused an already brimming cup to spill over. The cup itself was filled by years of silent structural wear, depleted salivary buffers, and low-level peripheral input.

### How Sufferers Achieve Remission

The liberating insight from Moseley and Butler's work is that **you do not need to empty every single drop from the cup to stop the pain.**

Treatment works through two complementary mechanisms:
1. **Draining Stressors from the Cup:** Removing physical triggers such as sealing unshielded root dentin, replacing broken filling margins, and neutralizing oral dietary acids.
2. **Expanding the Cup:** Down-regulating the central nervous system through restorative sleep, autonomic breathing, and reassurance that the mouth is structurally safe.

Bringing the total fluid level just a few millimeters below your nervous system's overflow threshold allows sensitized trigeminal pathways to quiet down, restoring daily comfort.
