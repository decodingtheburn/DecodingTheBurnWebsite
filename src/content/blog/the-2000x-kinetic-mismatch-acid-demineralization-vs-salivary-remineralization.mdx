Most conventional dental guidance repeats the reassuring idea that human saliva naturally protects and repairs your teeth. We are told that saliva buffers acidity, washes away food debris, and bathes hydroxyapatite crystals in calcium and phosphate to rebuild lost minerals.

While this biophysical buffering mechanism is real, looking closely at the kinetic rates reveals an alarming mathematical truth that rarely gets discussed in clinical dental appointments.

Demineralization and remineralization do not operate on an equal playing field. When you compare the actual speed of acid erosion against the maximum physiological speed of salivary remineralization, you discover an astonishing **2,000-to-1 kinetic mismatch**.

## Why the Math Breaks Down in Real Life

If salivary repair is over 2,000 times slower than acid erosion, how does anyone keep their teeth?

In an ideal ancestral environment, acid exposure was rare, brief, and followed by long periods of neutral chewing. But modern diets create an unbroken barrage of chemical challenges:

### 1. Frequent Acid Sipping Destroys the Balance
Sipping on coffee, lemon water, flavored seltzers, kombucha, or diet sodas throughout the morning does not count as a single event. Every sip resets the Stephan curve, plunging intra-oral pH into the demineralization zone for 20 to 40 minutes. 

Because remineralization is 2,000 times slower, your saliva never gets enough uninterrupted neutral time to catch up. You run a persistent daily mineral deficit.

### 2. The Root Dentin Vulnerability (pH 6.2 vs. 5.5)
Enamel is the hardest mineral in the human body and resists dissolution down to pH 5.5. But when gum recession or crown cement washout exposes the root neck (cervical dentin and cementum), the critical threshold rises to **pH 6.2 to 6.7**.

Root dentin dissolves at near-neutral pH. Mild acids that wouldn't touch crown enamel will rapidly strip root dentin at the full 116 µm/hour rate, unroofing thousands of microscopic fluid-filled tubules.

### 3. The 72-Hour Subgingival Fermentation Pump
The greatest danger is not just direct dietary acid in the mouth. When fermentable carbohydrates (sugars, starches, flours) get trapped in 3 mm to 5 mm periodontal pockets or under compromised crown margins, anaerobic bacteria (*Streptococcus mutans*, *Lactobacillus*) ferment them into concentrated subgingival lactic acid.

This subgingival fermentation pump runs 24 hours a day directly against vulnerable root dentin. Because saliva cannot penetrate deep subgingival crevices to buffer this localized acid, the 116 µm/hour dissolution continues unchecked beneath the gumline.

## What This Means for Burning Mouth Syndrome (BMS)

In Burning Mouth Syndrome, patients suffer relentless scalding pain despite oral mucosa appearing visually pink and healthy. The 2,000x kinetic mismatch explains the hidden biophysical driver:

1. **Tubules Are Striped Open:** The fast demineralization rate hollows out exposed root tubules at nearly 2 microns per minute, stripping away peritubular mineral plugs.
2. **Slow Remineralization Fails to Re-Seal Them:** Natural salivary minerals cannot re-crystallize fast enough to close active microscopic leaks.
3. **Acid Protons Infiltrate the Pulp:** With open conduits leading directly to pulpal mechanoreceptors, ambient acids flood the nerve endings.
4. **Nerve Alarms Stay Switched On:** Acid-Sensing Ion Channels (ASIC3) and TRPV1 receptors fire continuously, creating central trigeminal convergence that the brain perceives as a scalding fire across the tongue and palate.

## The Practical Strategy: You Cannot Remineralize Your Way Out of an Active Flood

Because salivary remineralization is mathematically 2,000 times slower than acid erosion, **trying to out-remineralize an acidic oral environment with remineralizing pastes or special rinses while continuing to consume acids is a losing battle.**

To protect your teeth and stop neuropathic oral burning, the order of operations must be respected:

1. **Stop the 116 µm/hour Dissolution First:** Eliminate exogenous dietary acids (zero coffee, citrus, vinegar, wine, soft drinks) and eliminate fermentable starches that feed subgingival lactic acid production.
2. **Mechanically Seal the Breaches:** Do not wait months for saliva to deposit fractions of a micron. Have your dentist physically seal open cervical tubules with composite resin patches, bonded restorations, and in-office Gluma protein coagulators.
3. **Protect Nocturnal Saliva:** Salivary flow naturally drops to near zero during sleep. Ensure nighttime hydration, mouth taping if prone to mouth-breathing, and complete absence of evening carbohydrate residues.
