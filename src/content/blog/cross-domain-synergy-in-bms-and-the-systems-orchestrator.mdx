## The 5 Domains Required to Solve BMS

### 1. Oral Anatomy & Restorative Dentistry
* **Focus:** Tooth geography, cementum integrity, cervical root recession, subgingival margins, composite filling leaks, and crown washouts.
* **Why it's needed:** Identifies the physical hardware leaks that expose dentinal tubules to the oral environment.

### 2. Peripheral & Central Neurobiology
* **Focus:** Small-fiber nerve demyelination, C-fiber density, trigeminal nerve pathways (CN V3), and central pain amplification in the brainstem.
* **Why it's needed:** Explains why sensitized nerve endings fire continuously in response to minor environmental shifts.

### 3. Biochemistry & Molecular Ion Channels
* **Focus:** ASIC3 (Acid-Sensing Ion Channel 3), TRPV1 heat receptors, TRPA1 chemical irritant channels, free hydrogen ion ($H^+$) dynamics, and titratable plant acids.
* **Why it's needed:** Uncovers the exact molecular mechanism of how acid triggers electrical action potentials in nerve membranes.

### 4. Oral Microbiome & Acid Fermentation
* **Focus:** Subgingival anaerobic bacteria (Streptococci, Lactobacilli), fermentable carbohydrates, FODMAP sugars, and localized lactic acid production.
* **Why it's needed:** Explains how non-acidic foods (like grains or vegetables) are transformed into localized acid in periodontal trenches.

### 5. Gastroenterology & Autonomic Physiology
* **Focus:** Salivary bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$) buffering, xerostomia, gastric acid micro-reflux ($HCl$), and sympathetic stress/cortisol surges.
* **Why it's needed:** Explains how stress, sleep apnea, or reflux alter oral pH and dismantle the mouth's natural chemical shields.

## Why Single-Domain Specialists Fail

Medical care is structured around deep domain specialization. A periodontist knows everything about bone levels around teeth, but rarely studies ASIC3 ion channel kinetics. A neurologist knows nerve-modulating drugs, but cannot evaluate a subgingival crown margin under magnification.

When a condition requires cross-domain integration, specialized medicine breaks down because **nobody owns the intersection**.

## The Profile of the Person Who Can Orchestrate This

Who is equipped to solve a cross-domain puzzle like BMS? 

It is rarely a traditional clinician working within a 15-minute appointment constraint. Instead, it requires a **Systems Orchestrator** with a specific mindset:

1. **Systems-Thinking & Engineering Discipline:** Usually someone trained in software engineering, systems architecture, or complex forensic data analysis who views problems as interconnected systems rather than isolated symptoms.
2. **First-Principles Thinking:** Refuses to accept "idiopathic" as an answer. Strips the problem down to basic physics, chemistry, and biology (e.g. *"What physical input causes a nerve cell to fire?"*).
3. **Data-Driven & Forensic Mindset:** Keeps rigorous elimination logs, maps exact tooth geography, audits time-series X-rays, and measures 0–10 pain metrics empirically.
4. **Relentless Patient Advocacy:** Has the personal skin in the game to act as the primary case manager, interviewing dentists and specialists to execute targeted diagnostic trials.

When a patient adopts the role of the Systems Orchestrator, they unite all five domains into a single, cohesive recovery strategy.
