## Why Triggers Vary Between Patients

The reason a specific food or habit triggers intense burning in one patient but causes zero reaction in another comes down to **micro-anatomical geography**:

1. **Depth of Root Exposure:** If recession or pocketing has exposed dentinal tubules along the lower molars, lateral tongue borders that rest against those teeth will react heavily to dietary acids (pH < 5.0).
2. **Salivary Buffer Capacity:** Patients with reduced un-stimulated salivary flow (xerostomia) lack the bicarbonate buffer needed to neutralize hydrogen ions, leading to prolonged acid contact time.
3. **Receptor Density:** Prior nerve trauma or chronic inflammation upregulates ASIC3 (Acid-Sensing Ion Channel 3) and TRPV1 receptors on mucosal C-fibers, making peripheral nerves fire at micro-concentrations of acid that normal tissue ignores.

## How to Test Your Personal Trigger Profile

Rather than eliminating everything at once without clear evidence, treat your triggers as a systematic single-variable experiment:

1. **Establish a Clean Baseline:** Follow a zero-acid, non-fermentable baseline for 7 to 14 days until your pain stabilizes at its lowest level.
2. **Re-Challenge One Item:** Reintroduce a single suspect item (e.g., 4 oz of coffee or a sparkling water) early in the day.
3. **Monitor Flare Response Time:** Record whether scalding pain ignites within 15 to 45 minutes. If it does, note it down in your forensic pain log.
