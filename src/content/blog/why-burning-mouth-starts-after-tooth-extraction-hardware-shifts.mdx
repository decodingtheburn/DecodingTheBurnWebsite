One of the most perplexing and frustrating mysteries in oral medicine is the patient who says:

> *"My mouth was completely fine my entire life. Then I had a single molar extracted. Six weeks later, the socket was fully healed and pink, but my tongue, palate, and gums started burning like an inferno. My dentist took X-rays and said everything looks 100% healed and normal."*

When a dentist evaluates an extraction site, their clinical checklist is straightforward:
* Is there an active alveolar osteitis (dry socket)? No.
* Is there a bone sequestration or residual root tip? No.
* Has the mucosal epithelial barrier closed cleanly over the alveolar ridge? Yes.

To the dental provider, the case is closed: the extraction was an absolute success. 

Yet the patient is living with an agonizing 8/10 or 10/10 scald. Because standard checkups look spotless, patients are frequently dismissed, told it is "psychosomatic," or referred out to a neurologist for systemic neuropathic pain pills.

When you analyze oral biomechanics from first principles, **the socket healing is only half the story**. Pulling a tooth radically alters the physical hardware and chemical micro-environment of the entire mouth. 

Here are the four unseen hardware shifts that turn a routine extraction into Burning Mouth Syndrome.

---

## 1. Unshielding Adjacent Root Collars: The Interdental Papilla Collapse

Teeth do not exist in isolation; they are locked into an architectural arch where contact points and shared interdental bone protect the delicate root necks of adjacent teeth.

When a tooth is extracted:
1. **The Interproximal Papilla Recedes:** The triangular gum tissue between the extracted tooth and its neighbor naturally shrinks and recedes as bone remodels.
2. **45,000 Patent Tubules Unsealed:** That minor 1 to 2 millimeter recession exposes the cervical collar of the neighboring tooth. Human dentin contains **20,000 to 45,000 microscopic fluid-filled tubules per square millimeter**, each leading directly to pulp nerve endings.
3. **No Gluma or Resin Seal Applied:** Dentists routinely seal the tooth they are treating, but rarely think to apply a dentin desensitizer (like Gluma or Tokuyama Shield Force Plus) to the exposed root neck of the neighbor tooth left standing next to the void.

The socket itself is healed, but the neighbor tooth now has thousands of open portals exposed to the oral cavity.

---

## 2. Occlusal Overload and Periodontal Ligament (PDL) Bruising

Every tooth in your mouth acts as a shock absorber. It sits in a sling of sensory-rich fibers called the **periodontal ligament (PDL)**.

When you remove one or two teeth from the arch:
* **The Fulcrum Shifts:** Biting forces of 150 to 250 pounds of pressure per square inch no longer distribute evenly across the dental arch.
* **Excursive Interferences & Occlusal Trauma:** During lateral jaw movements and nocturnal grinding, the teeth immediately adjacent to the gap become unnatural mechanical fulcrums. They absorb heavy, off-axis lateral shear forces.
* **Periodontal Bruising:** This primary occlusal trauma literally bruises the PDL fibers around neighboring teeth, creating continuous, subclinical neurogenic inflammation.
* **Cervical Abfraction:** The mechanical bending forces cause microscopic enamel crystal flaking at the cervical margin (abfraction), popping open even more dentinal tubules right at the gumline.

---

## 3. The Anaerobic Food Trap and Localized Micro-Acidification

An extraction space, even after mucosal closure, creates a structural depression in the dental arch. 

* **Salivary Stagnation:** Without a tight tooth-to-tooth contact point, food particles, carbohydrate fibers, and mucosal debris naturally accumulate in the interdental recess.
* **Biofilm Fermentation:** Oral bacteria (such as *Streptococcus mutans* and *Lactobacillus*) rapidly ferment these trapped carbohydrates, producing concentrated localized pools of **lactic acid**.
* **The pH Plunge:** While resting whole saliva may test at a safe pH of 7.0, the localized fluid sitting against that receded neighbor tooth can plunge to **pH 5.0 to 5.5**.

This chronic acidic reservoir sits directly against unshielded dentin and the lateral border of the resting tongue.

---

## 4. The Trigeminal ASIC3 Window Current: An Alarm That Cannot Shut Off

Why doesn't the oral cavity adapt to this localized acid?

As detailed in sensory neurobiology research, trigeminal nerve endings in the tongue and oral mucosa are packed with **ASIC3 (Acid-Sensing Ion Channel 3)** at densities up to 10 times higher than body skin.

* **Receptors That Adapt:** In most body tissues, pain channels like ASIC1a or TRPV1 open briefly when acid touches them and rapidly shut down (desensitize) within 20 to 30 seconds.
* **The ASIC3 Anomaly:** Cryo-EM structural research demonstrates that ASIC3 lacks this rapid desensitization hinge. When exposed to mild, chronic acidity between **pH 6.2 and 6.7**, ASIC3 channels get locked open in a continuous **"sustained window current."**
* **The Unbroken Fire:** The acidic biofilm from the extraction pocket continuously washes over exposed adjacent root tubules. Sodium ions pour uninterrupted into trigeminal sensory axons, generating action potentials that register as continuous burning pain across the tongue and palate.

## What Needs to Be Done Differently?

Understanding this first-principles cascade transforms post-extraction management:

1. **Inspect Neighboring Teeth, Not Just the Socket:** Examine the adjacent teeth for cervical root exposure, enamel abfraction, or defective restorative margins with an explorer or endodontic microscope.
2. **Seal All Exposed Neighbor Tubules Immediately:** Dentists should routinely apply a multi-layer biocompatible resin sealer (such as Gluma, Tokuyama Shield Force Plus, or Immediate Dentin Sealing resins) to all adjacent exposed root collars.
3. **Equilibrate Occlusion:** Check for heavy contacts or excursive interferences on neighboring teeth using 8-micron articulating film to eliminate PDL trauma and mechanical tooth flexion.
4. **Alkalinize and Neutralize the Micro-Environment:** Eliminate dietary acid washes and use buffered saline or sodium bicarbonate rinses to pull resting oral pH well above the ASIC3 activation threshold (pH 6.8+).

The extraction didn't cut a nerve, and the burning is not in your head. It is a predictable biomechanical and chemical chain reaction on the teeth left behind.
