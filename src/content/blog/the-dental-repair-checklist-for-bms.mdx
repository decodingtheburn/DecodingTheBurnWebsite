When investigating chronic Burning Mouth Syndrome, clinicians frequently overlook localized mechanical and hydrodynamic triggers because the soft oral mucosa appears visually normal. However, microscopic breaches in tooth structure allow dietary acids and bacterial fermentation products to penetrate exposed dentin, stimulating pulpal mechanoreceptors and activating acid-sensing ion channels (ASIC).

This checklist outlines the three key physical dental repairs to evaluate and address with your dental team, ordered by practical clinical priority.

---

## 1. Cervical Patches (Sealing Exposed Root Dentin)

Gingival recession exposes vulnerable root surfaces below the cementoenamel junction (CEJ). Unlike enamel, which resists demineralization down to pH 5.5, exposed root dentin begins dissolving at near-neutral pH (6.2 to 6.7).

When root dentin is exposed, thousands of microscopic dentinal tubules become open conduits directly to pulpal nerve fibers. Ambient dietary acids and subgingival lactic acid pool in these cervical areas, causing persistent fluid shifts and chronic neural firing.

### Action Steps for Cervical Patches:
- **Visual & Tactile Mapping:** Have your dentist carefully explore cervical margins with an explorer to identify abfraction notches, wear facets, and exposed yellow root dentin.
- **Biomimetic Resin Sealing:** Place thin, polished micro-hybrid or flowable composite resin patches directly over exposed dentin along the gumline to create an impermeable physical barrier.
- **Occlusal Assessment:** Evaluate for occlusal interference and clenching forces, which cause flexure at the tooth neck and accelerate cervical enamel breakdown.

## 2. The Leaky Crown (Margin Degradation & Micro-Leakage)

A classic blind spot in chronic BMS investigations is the aging dental crown. Even when bitewing X-rays show no overt recurrent caries, crown margins can suffer microscopic cement washout, micro-fractures, or marginal discrepancy.

Acidic fluids and anaerobic bacteria seep beneath defective crown margins, establishing a continuous subgingival fermentation chamber. This localized acid bath constantly stimulates the underlying dental pulp, radiating diffuse burning pain along the trigeminal pathway.

### Action Steps for Crown Evaluation:
- **Exploratory Margin Check:** Run an explorer probe 360 degrees around every crown margin, checking specifically for catches, voids, or soft cement borders.
- **Cold & Air Isolation Testing:** Test individual crowned teeth with targeted cold air to check for sharp pulpal responses indicative of open margins.
- **Definitive Fix (Refit or Replacement):** If margin integrity is compromised, the crown must be carefully removed, underlying dentin disinfected and sealed (Immediate Dentin Sealing), and replaced with a precision-bonded restoration.

## 3. In-Office Gluma (Deep Tubule Protein Occlusion)

For widespread exposed root surfaces, microscopic craze lines, or areas where composite patches cannot immediately be placed, professional desensitizing agents provide direct biochemical occlusion.

Gluma Desensitizer (a combination of glutaraldehyde and HEMA) works through a distinct mechanism: glutaraldehyde coagulates serum proteins within the dentinal fluid, forming transverse protein septa inside the tubules. HEMA then facilitates deep penetration, creating immediate physical plugs that block fluid displacement and acid ingress.

### Action Steps for Gluma Application:
- **Isolation:** Carefully isolate and protect gingival soft tissue before application, as glutaraldehyde can irritate soft mucosa.
- **Direct Application Protocol:** Apply Gluma to clean, dry dentin for 30 to 60 seconds, allow it to penetrate the tubules, gently dry with air, and rinse thoroughly.
- **Diagnostic Confirmation:** If applying Gluma immediately dampens or eliminates your background oral burning over the following 24 to 48 hours, it provides compelling diagnostic evidence that your BMS has a primary hydrodynamic and dental hardware origin.

## Summary Checklist for Your Next Appointment

1. [ ] **Inspect Cervical Margins:** Identify all receded root surfaces and schedule cervical composite bonding.
2. [ ] **Audit Crown Margins:** Probe every existing crown margin for cement breakdown or micro-leakage.
3. [ ] **Apply In-Office Gluma:** Seal sensitive or exposed root areas to coagulate tubule fluid and test symptom response.
