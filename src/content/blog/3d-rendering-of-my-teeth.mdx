## What the 3D Scan Shows: Connecting Geometry to Nerve Pain

Exploring my teeth in three dimensions immediately revealed three physical patterns that flat examinations failed to put into context:

### 1. Tooth #36: The Hidden Distal Crown Gap
On the lower left quadrant (viewable in the **Mandibular Arch** and **Left Lateral** views), sits tooth #36. For 20 years, visual checkups described this porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM) crown as "intact." 

However, in 3D, you can see how the restoration interfaces with the distal gumline. Periodontal charting confirmed a **defective distal joint**, and independent radiograph audits uncovered **three hidden cavities** eroding dentin underneath. 

Because that gap sat subgingivally, it created a permanent anaerobic incubator: pumping bacterial lactic acid directly into exposed dentinal tubules and the inferior alveolar nerve pathway (trigeminal branch V3).

### 2. Canines (Tooth #23 & #13): 1mm Cervical Recession & 10/10 Allodynia
In the **Frontal Bite** and **Maxillary Arch** views, notice the cervical margins along the upper canines. The clinical note records:
> *"23B récession 1mm... Douleur avec soie au niveau des canines... L’intensité de l'inconfort au passage du fil de soie dentaire est de 10/10 et dure quelques secondes."*

While a 1mm gum recession is often dismissed as "minor" by standard dental guidelines, in a patient with sensitized Acid-Sensing Ion Channels (ASIC3), that 1mm gap exposes thousands of conical dentinal tubules. Passing dental floss physically disturbed those exposed nerve endings, sending a 10/10 shockwave through the maxillary trigeminal branch (V2).

### 3. Teeth #24 & #26: Overcontoured Buccal Composites
In the clinical notes, the hygienist and dentist documented:
> *"24B composite avec surcontour... 26B composite en surcontour."*

When resin fillings are placed with an overcontour (a bulky protruding ledge), they trap food debris and plaque along the gingival margin. This chronic micro-plaque accumulation keeps local gum tissue in a state of continuous inflammatory vasodilation, lowering tissue pH and preventing the mucosal barrier from ever closing.

## Cross-Referencing the 3D Scan with the Florida Probe Chart

To understand why the 3D scan is so valuable, you have to look at it alongside my **Florida Probe periodontal chart** recorded on April 14, 2025:

| Diagnostic Metric | Clinical Finding | Neuropathic Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pocket Depths** | 2mm - 4mm throughout (mostly 3mm) | Pockets are shallow enough to rule out active periodontitis, but deep enough to trap acid-producing biofilm. |
| **Bleeding on Probing (BOP)** | 9% of sites (15 total sites) | Mild, localized bleeding: standard cleaning did not stop the burning fire. |
| **Probing Sensation** | *"Le sondage fut excessivement difficile et sensible"* | Probing was excruciatingly painful despite minimal tissue breakdown, confirming **severe hyperalgesia**. |
| **Restoration Status** | *"Restauration défectueuse #36D"* | Confirms a mechanical gap directly adjacent to the most painful mandibular zone. |

The periodontist explicitly noted in her report:
> *"La condition parodontale ne peut expliquer l’ampleur des inconforts ressentis par M. Éthier."*  
> *(The periodontal condition cannot explain the magnitude of discomfort felt by Mr. Ethier.)*

This is the central paradox of Burning Mouth Syndrome: **the physical tissue damage does not match the agony felt by the patient**. 

A standard dentist looks at these 3D scans and sees mild wear, a few composites, and modest 1mm recessions. But when those physical defects occur in a nervous system already primed by chronic oral inflammation and acid hypersensitivity, they act as active physical triggers that keep the fire burning.

## Why Every BMS Patient Should Request Their 3D Scan Files

If your dental clinic uses an intraoral digital scanner (like iTero, TRIOS, or CEREC), those 3D files (`.STL` or `.OBJ` format) belong to you:

1. **They provide an objective visual baseline:** You can track recession progression, enamel wear facets, and restoration margins across time.
2. **You can get independent second opinions:** You can share the 3D file or clear multi-angle screenshots with biological dentists, airway specialists, and periodontists worldwide.
3. **They eliminate guesswork:** Instead of wondering where your pain maps to, you can visually correlate your burning hotspots to specific crowns, fillings, and receding margins.

When we combine 3D digital anatomy with time-series symptom tracking, we stop guessing about Burning Mouth Syndrome: and start decoding the burn.
