## The Four Mechanical Triggers Mapped in&nbsp;3D

Examining the dental arch in three dimensions makes it clear that my oral burning was not an "imagined" or purely psychosomatic sensation. It mapped directly to four distinct categories of physical tissue defects:

### 1. Periodontal Pockets &amp; Severe Probing&nbsp;Hyperalgesia
On my **Florida Probe periodontal audit** recorded on April 14, 2025, the clinician documented:
* Pocket depths ranging from **2mm to 4mm** (predominantly 3mm sulcular depths throughout).
* **9% Bleeding on Probing (BOP)** across 15 separate anatomical sites.
* Probing sensation: *"Le sondage fut excessivement difficile et sensible"* (probing was agonizingly painful).

The periodontist explicitly noted:
> *"La condition parodontale ne peut expliquer l’ampleur des inconforts ressentis par M. Éthier."*  
> *(The periodontal condition cannot explain the magnitude of discomfort felt by Mr. Ethier.)*

While a 3mm or 4mm pocket is not advanced periodontitis from a bone-loss perspective, it is deep enough to trap anaerobic bacteria. In a patient whose oral nociceptors are primed in a state of hyperalgesia, routine periodontal probing triggers an excruciating neural flare, proving that peripheral nerve endings were severely&nbsp;sensitized.

### 2. Cervical Recessions &amp; 10/10 Floss&nbsp;Allodynia
In the maxillary arch, notice the prominent cervical collars on the canines (teeth #13 and #23):
* **Tooth #23**: The clinical note documents *"23B récession 1mm... L’intensité de l’inconfort au passage du fil de soie dentaire est de 10/10 et dure quelques secondes."*
* **Tooth #13**: Bilateral flossing tenderness along the exposed cementoenamel&nbsp;junction.

A 1mm gum recession is routinely considered "clinically minor" by conventional dentists. However, even a 1mm loss of gingival tissue strips away the protective enamel shield, exposing thousands of microscopic conical dentinal tubules. When dental floss mechanically rubs across those exposed tubules in a patient with up-regulated Acid-Sensing Ion Channels (ASIC3), it triggers a severe 10/10 shockwave straight into the maxillary branch of the trigeminal nerve&nbsp;(V2).

### 3. Abfractions &amp; Non-Carious Cervical Enamel&nbsp;Notches
Across the premolars (teeth #14, #15, #24, #25 in the upper arch, and #34, #35 in the lower arch), the 3D model highlights cervical abfraction&nbsp;lesions:
* **Mechanism**: Eccentric bite stress, grinding, and clenching cause the teeth to flex microscopically at the gumline.
* Over years, this mechanical stress causes brittle enamel rods at the cementoenamel junction to break away, leaving sharp wedge-shaped notches (abfractions).
* Once that enamel is lost, dentin is directly exposed to oral fluids. When you consume acidic foods or carbonated beverages, hydrogen ions ($H^+$) penetrate directly into the dentinal fluid, causing instant burning and sharp electric nerve&nbsp;pain.

### 4. Restorations: Defective Crowns &amp; Overcontoured&nbsp;Composites
Restorative work that is poorly sealed or overcontoured creates chronic mechanical and microbial irritation:
* **Tooth #36 (Lower Left First Molar)**: Periodontal charting revealed a **defective distal crown margin** (*"#36D joint au distal"*), while an independent X-ray audit discovered **three hidden recurrent cavities** eroding dentin underneath. This created a permanent anaerobic bacteria incubator pumping lactic acid directly into the inferior alveolar nerve pathway (trigeminal branch&nbsp;V3).
* **Teeth #24 &amp; #26 (Upper Premolar &amp; Molar)**: Dental audit records note *"24B composite avec surcontour... 26B composite en surcontour."* Overcontoured composite resin ledges trap plaque and food debris right against the delicate marginal gingiva, keeping the tissue in continuous inflammatory vasodilation and preventing the epithelial shield from ever&nbsp;closing.

## Why Mapping Issues in 3D Resolves the BMS&nbsp;Paradox

In Burning Mouth Syndrome, patients are frequently told: *"Your mouth looks mostly normal. We cannot find anything wrong."*

Flat mirrors and 2D bite-wing X-rays hide how these defects work together:
1. **The Broken Shield**: Recessions, abfractions, and defective margins strip away the protective epithelial and enamel barrier.
2. **The Chronic Acid Incubator**: Overcontoured fillings and subgingival crown gaps trap acidic biofilm that normal saliva cannot neutralize.
3. **The Fired-Up Nerves**: Acids seep into microscopic gaps, bathing hypersensitized nerve endings and triggering non-stop neuropathic burning.

When you view the entire arch in three dimensions, the mystery disappears. The burning pain is not invisible: it is directly anchored to identifiable mechanical defects across the dental&nbsp;arch.
