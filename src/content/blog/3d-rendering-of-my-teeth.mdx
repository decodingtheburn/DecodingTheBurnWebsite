## The Key Clinical Findings Mapped in&nbsp;3D

Examining the dental arch in three dimensions makes it clear that my oral burning was not an "imagined" or purely psychosomatic sensation. It mapped directly to identifiable physical tissue defects documented across multiple independent dental examinations:

### 1. Floss Pain &amp; Mechanical Allodynia Zones
When oral nociceptors are primed in a state of central and peripheral sensitization, routine flossing turns from a simple hygiene task into agonizing neuropathic flares. In the 3D model, three distinct flossing trigger zones are&nbsp;mapped:
* **Tooth #23 (Upper Left Canine)**: Periodontal notes recorded: *"23B récession 1mm... L’intensité de l’inconfort au passage du fil de soie dentaire est de 10/10 et dure quelques secondes."* Passing dental floss across exposed cervical dentinal tubules generated an excruciating 10/10 shockwave straight into the maxillary branch of the trigeminal nerve&nbsp;(V2).
* **Tooth #13 (Upper Right Canine)**: Bilateral flossing tenderness recorded along the exposed cervical cementoenamel junction with a thin alveolar gingival&nbsp;biotype.
* **Interdental Contact #33-#34 (Lower Left Embrasure)**: Patient-reported severe floss sensitivity and snagging between lower left canine #33 and first premolar #34. Florida Probe periodontal charting documented **inadequate attached gingiva on #34** and minimal attached gingiva on #33. Deficient keratinized tissue leaves unmyelinated periosteal nerve endings unprotected, allowing mechanical floss pressure to directly compress sensitized branches of the inferior alveolar nerve&nbsp;(V3).

### 2. Five Confirmed Cervical Composite Patches &amp; Defective Crown
A comprehensive audit of Dr. Matthieu Ménard's clinical examination confirmed that **tooth #11 (upper right central incisor)** has a Class V cervical composite patch covering gum recession (*"11B composite avec récession"*). In total, exactly **five cervical composite patches** and one defective crown margin were documented across my&nbsp;mouth:
1. **Tooth #11 (Upper Right Central Incisor)**: Buccal Class V composite patch bonded over 1mm gum recession.
2. **Tooth #16 (Upper Right First Molar)**: Buccal Class V composite patch along the gingival margin.
3. **Tooth #24 (Upper Left First Premolar)**: Buccal composite with documented overcontour (*"24B composite avec surcontour"*).
4. **Tooth #26 (Upper Left First Molar)**: Buccal composite with documented overcontour (*"26B composite en surcontour"*).
5. **Tooth #44 (Lower Right First Premolar)**: Buccal composite patch with slight marginal overcontour (*"44B compo avec léger surcontour"*).
6. **Tooth #36 (Lower Left First Molar)**: Defective distal crown margin (*"#36D joint au distal"*) with three hidden recurrent cavities undermining dentin, acting as a permanent anaerobic bacterial acid&nbsp;trap.

### 3. Florida Probe Recessions &amp; Attached Gingiva Deficits
A 1mm gum recession is often dismissed as "minor" by conventional dentistry, but even a 1mm loss of marginal gingiva strips away protective enamel and exposes thousands of microscopic dentinal tubules. The Florida Probe chart confirmed 1mm recessions across multiple&nbsp;teeth:
* **Maxillary Recessions**: Tooth #23 (1mm recession charted across mesial, buccal, and distal surfaces: code `1 1 1`), tooth #24 (`0 1 0`), tooth #11 (recession under composite patch), tooth #17 (`0 1 0`), and tooth #27 (`1 1 1`).
* **Mandibular Recessions**: Anterior incisors #31 and #32 (`0 1 0`), and terminal molar #47 (`0 1 1`).
* **Attached Gingiva Status**: Inadequate attached gingiva on tooth #34, and minimal attached gingiva documented on teeth #11, #23, #24, #26, #33, #35, and&nbsp;#36.

### 4. Periodontal Pockets with Exact 4mm Depths Charted
On the Florida Probe audit recorded on April 14, 2025, the clinician documented:
* Pocket depths ranging from **2mm to 4mm** (predominantly 3mm sulcular depths throughout).
* Specific **4mm pocket sites** documented on **#36** (4mm distal and lingual), **#37** (4mm lingual), **#46** (4mm lingual), **#47** (4mm lingual), **#16** (4mm mesiobuccal and palatal), and **#27** (4mm distobuccal).
* **9% Bleeding on Probing (BOP)** across 15 separate bleeding sites.
* Probing agony: *"Le sondage fut excessivement difficile et sensible... La condition parodontale ne peut expliquer l’ampleur des inconforts ressentis par M. Éthier."*

While 4mm pockets do not indicate advanced bone loss, they are deep enough to host anaerobic pathogens (P. gingivalis, T. denticola) that produce organic acids. In a patient with primed trigeminal nociceptors, bacterial toxins in these pockets trigger intense burning&nbsp;sensations.

### 5. Bilateral Premolar Abfractions &amp; Occlusal Wear Notches
Across all four premolar quadrants (teeth #14 &amp; #15, #24 &amp; #25, #34 &amp; #35, and #44 &amp; #45), the 3D model documents bilateral cervical abfraction&nbsp;lesions:
* **Biomechanical Mechanism**: Teeth flex under lateral clenching and nocturnal grinding forces. Maximal tensile stress concentrates at the cervical fulcrum, snapping brittle enamel rods away from the tooth collar.
* **The BMS Pain Connection**: Once cervical enamel fractures, bare dentinal tubules contact dietary acids, hot liquids, and oral bacteria, firing sharp electrical pain signals directly into trigeminal branches V2 and&nbsp;V3.

### 6. Normal Restorations as Stable Control Sites
The model also maps teeth with normal, intact restorations (teeth #46, #17, #27, and #37). Comparing these stable restorations against defective sites like #36 proves that well-sealed dental work without marginal breakdown or overcontour does not generate localized neuropathic&nbsp;irritation.

## Why Mapping Issues in 3D Resolves the BMS&nbsp;Paradox

In Burning Mouth Syndrome, patients are frequently told: *"Your mouth looks mostly normal. We cannot find anything wrong."*

Flat mirrors and 2D bite-wing X-rays hide how these defects work together:
1. **The Broken Shield**: Recessions, abfractions, and defective margins strip away the protective epithelial and enamel barrier.
2. **The Chronic Acid Incubator**: Overcontoured fillings and subgingival crown gaps trap acidic biofilm that normal saliva cannot neutralize.
3. **The Fired-Up Nerves**: Acids seep into microscopic gaps, bathing hypersensitized nerve endings and triggering non-stop neuropathic burning.

When you view the entire arch in three dimensions, the mystery disappears. The burning pain is not invisible: it is directly anchored to identifiable mechanical defects across the dental&nbsp;arch.
