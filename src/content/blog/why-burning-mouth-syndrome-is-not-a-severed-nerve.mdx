A common fear among patients newly diagnosed with Burning Mouth Syndrome (BMS) is that a dentist, oral surgeon, or dental hygienist physically cut, severed, or crushed a major cranial nerve trunk during an extraction, implant placement, or deep root scaling.

When you feel a severe, unrelenting 8/10 or 10/10 scald across your tongue and palate every waking hour, your brain naturally imagines severe structural destruction: *“Someone must have severed a major nerve branch in my jaw.”*

However, when you analyze oral neuroanatomy and neurophysiology from first principles, **100% of classic Primary Burning Mouth Syndrome cases cannot be caused by a severed nerve**. 

Here is the exact clinical science, the diagnostic criteria, and the peer-reviewed evidence proving why.

---

## 1. Severed Nerves Cause "Anesthesia Dolorosa" or Numbness, Not Bilateral Burning

When a major peripheral nerve trunk (such as the lingual nerve or inferior alveolar nerve) is mechanically severed or crushed, the physical continuity of the axon is broken.

In neurology, complete transection produces an immediate, textbook clinical picture:
1. **Total Loss of Sensation (Anesthesia):** You cannot feel touch, pressure, pinpricks, or temperature on the area supplied by that nerve. If you run a cotton swab or explorer across that side of the tongue, it feels completely dead, exactly like a novocaine injection that never wore off.
2. **Unilateral Distribution:** Cranial nerves do not cross the anatomical midline. A severed lingual nerve on the right side produces total sensory loss strictly on the right half of the tongue. The left side remains 100% normal.
3. **Anesthesia Dolorosa (Painful Numbness):** When a severed nerve does cause burning, it is clinically termed **anesthesia dolorosa** ("pain in a numb area"). The patient feels severe central deafferentation burning or phantom aching, **yet the tissue remains completely numb to light touch**.

### How Primary BMS Completely Contradicts a Severed Nerve:
* **Sensory Perception Is Intact or Heightened:** In Burning Mouth Syndrome, your tongue is **not numb**. If a clinician touches your tongue with a light monofilament or thermal probe, you feel it immediately. In fact, many patients have **hyperalgesia** (heightened pain) or **allodynia** (innocuous touches register as painful burns).
* **Bilateral Symmetry:** BMS almost always presents bilaterally (both sides of the tongue tip and lateral borders, and frequently the hard palate and inner lip). A surgeon would have had to sever symmetrical nerve trunks on both sides of your face simultaneously, which does not happen during routine unilateral dental work.

## 2. Histology Proves the Nerves Are Present and Sprouting, Not Dead

In 2005, Dr. Giuseppe Lauria and colleagues published a landmark histological study in the journal *PAIN* evaluating tongue biopsies from patients with burning mouth syndrome ([Lauria et al. 2005, PubMed 15927393](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15927393/)).

Using epithelial punch biopsies of the anterior tongue stained with **PGP 9.5** (protein gene product 9.5, an axonal marker), Lauria made a fundamental discovery:
* BMS patients showed a significant loss of intraepithelial nerve fibers (IENF) in the topmost mucosal layer.
* However, the remaining subepithelial nerve fibers were **structurally intact, actively alive, and exhibiting axonal sprouting**.

These are not severed stumps trapped in bone; they are live, unmyelinated C-fibers and thin A-delta fibers sitting just sub-microns beneath the oral skin. Because their protective myelin and epithelial cushion are thinned, their bare membrane receptors are directly exposed to the chemical environment of the mouth.

## 3. Traumatic Neuromas Create Focal "Tinel Signs," Not Diffuse Burning

When a peripheral nerve is physically severed or lacerated in the mouth, the injured proximal stump attempts to regenerate. When it encounters dense scar tissue or bone and cannot reconnect, it forms a tangled ball of disorganized axons known as a **traumatic neuroma**.

A traumatic neuroma has very specific, unmistakable clinical signs:
* **A Palpable, Exquisitely Tender Nodule:** A neuroma typically forms a localized lump in the retromolar pad or lingual sulcus where the surgical insult occurred.
* **Positive Tinel's Sign:** Tapping on that specific millimeter spot sends an electric shock straight through the nerve path.
* **No Diffuse Mucosal Scalding:** Neuromas do not cause a smooth, burning sheet of fire across the entire palate, inner lower lip, and tongue tip.

---

## 4. The Real Culprit: Chemical Gating of ASIC3 and ASIC1a

If the nerve is not severed, why does it burn non-stop?

This is where ion channel biophysics solves the mystery that standard medicine missed:
1. **The Intact Nerve Ending:** Your tongue's sensory fibers are alive, healthy enough to transmit signals, but stripped of their protective surface barrier.
2. **The Trigeminal Anomaly:** Trigeminal sensory terminals in the mouth express **ASIC3** (Acid-Sensing Ion Channel 3) at densities 5 to 10 times higher than body skin.
3. **The Missing Shutoff Timer:** While skin receptors (like ASIC1a or TRPV1) rapidly desensitize within seconds, **ASIC3 maintains a non-inactivating "window current"** at mild acidic pH levels between 6.2 and 6.7.
4. **The Daily Proton Wash:** When exposed tooth root collars, dissolving dental luting cements, bacterial biofilm fermentation, or dietary beverages bathe these live, unshielded fibers in mild acidity, ASIC3 channels get locked in a continuous open state. 

Sodium continuously pours into the live nerve, forcing it to fire non-stop action potentials straight to the trigeminal brainstem nucleus.

## Key Clinical Distinctions

| Feature | Severed Lingual Nerve (Deafferentation) | Traumatic Neuroma | Primary Burning Mouth Syndrome (BMS) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tactile Sensation** | Total numbness (complete anesthesia) | Numb downstream, focal trigger point | **Intact or hypersensitive (allodynia)** |
| **Symmetry** | 100% Unilateral (one side only) | Focal spot (retromolar or extraction site) | **Bilateral (both sides of tongue, palate, lips)** |
| **Physical Exam** | Measurable sensory defect on QST | Palpable, tender submucosal nodule | **Visually normal, intact mucosa** |
| **Underlying Mechanism** | Structural transection of axon trunk | Entangled regenerating axons in scar tissue | **Small-fiber receptor depolarization (ASIC3 / ASIC1 / TRPV1)** |
| **Response to Local Acid** | None (severed fibers cannot transmit) | Variable | **Immediate flare or sustained burning** |

---

## The Takeaway

If you are suffering from Burning Mouth Syndrome, your nerves are **not severed, dead, or permanently destroyed**. 

They are **hyper-functional, irritated, and unshielded**. They are sounding a continuous biological alarm because microscopic physical hardware leaks, receded root margins, and dietary acids are continuously tripping ion channels that were never designed to handle an acidic oral bath. 

When you eliminate the chemical proton triggers and restore protective peripheral barriers, the fire finally gets the chance to go out.
