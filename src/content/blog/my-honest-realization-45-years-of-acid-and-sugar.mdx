If I am going to share this journey openly, I want to be completely honest about how I got&nbsp;here.

I am writing this today from a place of deep gratitude because right now, I am completely pain-free. But I know the burning pain of BMS. I know it all too well, and my daily wish is to see people in our community find relief in whatever way, shape, or form it can&nbsp;take.

I do not know what the future holds. I am still learning every single day, but looking back at my past 45 years helped me connect the pieces of my dental history and my&nbsp;condition.

---

<div class="part-label">Part 1</div>

## The 45-Year Silent Build-Up

For the first 45 years of my life, I lived like most people around me. I drank fruit juices, soft drinks, and alcohol, and I ate plenty of sweet, sugary&nbsp;foods.

I had no idea how damaging those daily habits were to my teeth. Even if someone had pulled me aside back then and told me that drinking acidic sodas and eating sugar was destroying my teeth, I probably would have kept going&nbsp;anyway.

Why? Because there was no pain. 

When your teeth look fine on the surface and nothing hurts, you assume everything is working properly. The damage was silent, microscopic, and cumulative year after&nbsp;year.

Every filling, crown, and root canal is a man-made restoration placed inside living biological tissue. They are necessary to save teeth, but they also leave microscopic margins, aging cement seams, and sensitive interfaces that become vulnerable as the years go&nbsp;by.

<div class="part-label">Part 3</div>

## The Risks of Doing Nothing vs. Doing Anything

Living through four years of Burning Mouth Syndrome has completely reshaped my perspective on dental interventions and daily&nbsp;habits.

Whenever you face dental issues, you realize there is a delicate trade-off:
* **The risk of doing nothing:** Untreated decay spreads, gum pockets deepen, and bacterial acid continues eroding vulnerable root dentin.
* **The risk of doing anything:** Every time a drill enters your mouth, an old filling is replaced, or a surgical procedure is performed, nerve pathways are stressed and tissue interfaces are altered.

Because of those risks, my primary goal today is to minimize the quantity of dental work I need in the future. I want to preserve the natural teeth and restorations I still have, rather than continually undergoing invasive repairs to fix new&nbsp;damage.

---

<div class="part-label">Part 4</div>

## Why I Would Rather Eat Carnivore for the Rest of My Life

People often ask me if sticking to a strict carnivore diet feels restrictive or difficult to&nbsp;sustain.

When I weigh eating an ordinary diet against the 10/10 agony of burning mouth pain, the answer is effortless. I would rather eat carnivore for the rest of my life than ever go through that fire&nbsp;again.

Eating meat, animal fats, and neutral foods removes 100% of the dietary acids and fermentable sugars that feed oral bacteria and trigger sensitized nerve endings. It protects my existing teeth, stops localized acid production in its tracks, and keeps my symptoms at a rock-solid&nbsp;zero.
