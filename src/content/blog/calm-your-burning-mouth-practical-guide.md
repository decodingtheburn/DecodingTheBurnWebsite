# Calm Your Burning Mouth: Practical Clinic Guide

A practical reference guide designed for use in the doctor and dentist office. When navigating Burning Mouth Syndrome (BMS), clinical conversations often stall because oral tissues look visually healthy while pain remains severe.

Use this structured guide to present clear symptoms, request high-yield diagnostic tests, and review proven empirical relief strategies.

---

## Part 1: How to Describe Your Symptoms

When consulting a physician or dentist, avoid vague statements like "my mouth hurts." Use precise clinical terminology that communicates neuropathic patterns:

1. **Bilateral Scalded Sensation**
   - Describe the exact sensation: severe scalding or sandpaper burning, predominantly on the anterior two-thirds of the tongue, hard palate, and inner lips.
   - Note that mucosal tissues appear visually pink and normal, with no ulcerations, white plaques, or erythema.

2. **The Diurnal Pain Curve**
   - Morning baseline: minimal or absent pain on waking (0 to 2 on a 10-point scale).
   - Progression: gradual escalation throughout the day, peaking in late afternoon or evening (7 to 9 on a 10-point scale).
   - This diurnal escalation is a hallmark of peripheral neuropathic exhaustion and cumulative chemical exposure.

3. **The 48 to 72 Hour Time Lag**
   - Flare-ups rarely occur instantaneously after eating.
   - Dietary irritants and fermentable carbohydrates take 48 to 72 hours to produce maximal lactic acid and trigger nerve firing.
   - Keeping a time-stamped food ledger reveals this delayed delta.

4. **Transient Relief While Eating**
   - Note whether chewing food or drinking cool liquids temporarily masks the burning.
   - Mechanoreceptor stimulation from mastication temporarily gates sensory pain transmission in the trigeminal pathways.

## Part 2: What Makes It Better

Empirical observations from patient ledgers and clinical reviews identify four key relief mechanisms:

### 1. Dietary Baseline Elimination
- **Zero Simple Sugars:** Eliminates the fuel oral bacteria (Streptococcus mutans) ferment into lactic acid.
- **Zero High-Acid Foods:** Eliminates direct chemical stimulation of Acid-Sensing Ion Channels (ASIC3) and TRPV1 receptors.
- **Strict Baseline Diet:** Pasture-raised meats, neutral animal fats, water, and non-acidic electrolytes provide an anti-inflammatory reset.

### 2. Sealing Exposed Dentinal Tubules
- Exposed cervical root margins (abfraction, gum recession) contain open dentinal tubules that act as direct chemical pipelines to pulp nerves.
- **Micro-Mechanical Bonding:** Glass ionomer or flowable composite seals placed over exposed margins block chemical ingress.
- **Occluding Toothpastes:** Daily application of 0.454% stannous fluoride or biomimetic nano-hydroxyapatite crystal deposits occlude tubule apertures.

### 3. Detergent Elimination
- Discontinuing foaming toothpastes containing Sodium Lauryl Sulfate (SLS).
- SLS denatures salivary mucin proteins, strips the protective epithelial barrier, and allows acidic penetration to sub-epithelial nerve endings.
- Switching to non-foaming, unflavored, or pure baking soda pastes protects oral tissue.

### 4. Saliva Buffering and Overnight Protection
- Saliva contains bicarbonate ions that neutralize microbial acids.
- During sleep, salivary flow drops by over 90%, leaving oral tissues unprotected.
- Rinsing with a mild sodium bicarbonate solution (half teaspoon baking soda in warm water) before sleep maintains an alkaline oral pH.
- Gentle mouth taping prevents mouth breathing, mucosal drying, and nocturnal acid pooling.

## Part 3: What Makes It Worse

Documented triggers that pull the neuropathic tripwire:

### 1. Chemical and Acidic Triggers
- **Citrus and Vinegars:** Lemons, limes, oranges, balsamic vinegar, and salad dressings directly lower oral pH below 5.5, activating ASIC channels.
- **Tomatoes and Sauces:** High citric and malic acid content directly irritates desensitized oral mucosa.
- **Carbonated Beverages:** Carbonic acid and phosphoric acid create prolonged acidic exposure across teeth and tongue.
- **Spicy Foods and Capsaicin:** Directly binds to TRPV1 receptors, producing prolonged burning sensations.

### 2. Fermentation Delay Culprits
- **Refined Carbohydrates and Bread:** Sticky starches lodge in gum pockets and between teeth, feeding biofilm for 48 to 72 hours.
- **Artificial Sweeteners:** Sorbitol, xylitol in excess, and sucralose can alter oral microflora and trigger mucosal irritation in sensitive individuals.

### 3. Mechanical Hardware Triggers
- **Cement Washout Around Old Crowns:** Gaps between crown margins and tooth preps trap decaying debris, creating local acid pumps.
- **Nocturnal Clenching and Grinding:** Heavy occlusal forces flex tooth necks, popping off cervical enamel and exposing sensitive root dentin.
- **Rough Restorations:** Sharp composite edges or chipped enamel create chronic micro-friction on tongue borders.

## Part 4: The Doctor Office Checklist

Bring this exact checklist to your primary care physician or neurologist:

```
[ ] Complete Blood Count (CBC) with differential
[ ] Serum Ferritin and Total Iron Binding Capacity (TIBC)
    * Target: Ferritin > 50-70 ng/mL for nerve myelination
[ ] Active B12 (Holotranscobalamin) and Serum Folate
    * Note: Standard serum B12 can read falsely normal
[ ] Serum Methylmalonic Acid (MMA) and Homocysteine
    * Functional markers of true cellular B12 deficiency
[ ] Serum Zinc and Copper Levels
[ ] Fasting Blood Glucose and HbA1c
    * Ruling out early diabetic or pre-diabetic small-fiber neuropathy
[ ] Thyroid Panel (TSH, Free T3, Free T4)
[ ] Autoimmune Serology (ANA, Anti-SSA / Ro, Anti-SSB / La)
    * Ruling out primary Sjögren's Syndrome
[ ] Medication Review
    * Screen for ACE inhibitors, diuretics, or anticholinergics causing xerostomia
```

### Script for Your Physician
> "I understand that my oral tissues look visually healthy. However, my symptoms match the established literature for peripheral small-fiber neuropathy of the trigeminal distribution. I would like to run a targeted lab panel to rule out systemic metabolic, autoimmune, or micronutrient deficiencies before considering psychotropic medications."

## Part 5: The Dentist Office Checklist

Bring this exact checklist to your general dentist or prosthodontist:

```
[ ] Tactile Explorer Audit of All Cervical Margins
    * Inspect for explorer catch, notch defects, and cement breakdown
[ ] Periapical and Bitewing Radiograph Review
    * Compare historical films to check for recurrent subgingival caries
[ ] Cold Air Stream Tubule Provocation Test
    * Test exposed root surfaces to pinpoint hyper-reactive teeth
[ ] Periodontal Sulcus Measurement
    * Check for isolated 4mm+ pockets harboring anaerobic biofilm
[ ] Occlusal Balance and Abfraction Check
    * Examine canine guidance and heavy lateral interference marks
[ ] Chemical Shielding Trial
    * Place temporary desensitizing varnish or resin seal on suspect margins
```

### Script for Your Dentist
> "I am not reporting general toothache. I am experiencing severe bilateral oral burning. Research shows that exposed dentinal tubules and margin micro-leakage act as chemical conduits to pulpal C-fibers, triggering central trigeminal sensitization. I would like us to systematically audit every crown margin and receded cervical root surface for micro-leakage, and consider sealing any exposed dentin."

## Part 6: The 6-Day Reset Protocol

When an accidental dietary exposure or dental procedure triggers a severe flare:

- **Days 1 to 2: Complete Elimination**
  - Return immediately to strict zero-carb, non-acidic whole foods (beef, salt, water).
  - Stop all toothpastes containing foaming agents; use pure water or baking soda.
  - Alkaline rinse (half teaspoon sodium bicarbonate in 8 oz warm water) 3 times daily.

- **Days 3 to 4: Tubule Occlusion**
  - Apply stannous fluoride or nano-hydroxyapatite paste directly to exposed margins with a clean fingertip. Leave undisturbed for 2 minutes before spitting.
  - Maintain gentle mouth taping at night to prevent mucosal desiccation.

- **Days 5 to 6: Stabilization Assessment**
  - Record morning and evening pain scores in your ledger.
  - Verify that the delayed delta begins dropping toward your calm baseline.
  - Log any new dental hardware adjustments or dietary deviations.
