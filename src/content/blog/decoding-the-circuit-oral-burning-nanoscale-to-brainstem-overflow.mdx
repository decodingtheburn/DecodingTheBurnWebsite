When your mouth feels as though it has been scalded with boiling liquid, standard medicine conducts an oral exam, notes clean pink mucosa, confirms intact bone levels on two-dimensional dental bitewings, and issues a standard diagnostic label: **Primary Idiopathic Burning Mouth Syndrome (BMS)**. 

The prevailing clinical narrative follows that because no macroscopic lesion is visible to an explorer probe or standard X-ray, the nerves must be misfiring spontaneously. A phantom software bug generated in a&nbsp;vacuum.

Nerves do not manufacture high-voltage alarms on their own. Primary sensory cables are passive transmission conduits. The signal always begins at the physical boundary: where external chemistry interacts with microscopic protein receptors embedded across the terminal tips of the&nbsp;line.

Mapping the complete biological signal path reveals that the human trigeminal system operates under strict physical bandwidth limits. It does not take an extensive structural lesion to overwhelm the circuit. A sub-micron breach is more than sufficient to saturate the pathway and trigger central spillover across the entire&nbsp;face.

```

   Sub-micron gap / unsealed dentinal tubules (20,000 - 45,000/mm²)

   Membrane potential reaches -55 mV threshold

   Analog input converted to binary spikes via Pulse-Frequency Modulation (PFM)
                              │
                              ▼
3. PERIPHERAL TRUNK TRANSMISSION
   Binary spikes travel along unmyelinated C-fibers & A-delta fibers
   Routed through Inferior Alveolar (V3) or Superior Alveolar (V2) trunks

```

## The Perimeter Interface: Unarmored Receptors vs. The External World

The body's sensory wiring is protected differently depending on the tissue interface. Fingertips and outer skin are shielded by twenty or more layers of dead, cornified cells (the *stratum corneum*) that prevent environmental fluids and acids from making physical contact with intraepidermal nerve&nbsp;endings.

In the oral cavity, that keratin armor is entirely absent across root cementum and sulcular gum pockets. Under every tooth crown, the **Subodontoblastic Plexus of Raschkow** concentrates 30,000 to 50,000 sensory terminals directly against porous&nbsp;dentin.

When gums recede or dental cements degrade, microscopic dentinal tubules become uncapped. Dentin contains **20,000 to 45,000 fluid-filled tubules per square millimeter**, adding up to over 100 million open microscopic tunnels across exposed root&nbsp;surfaces.

## Transduction: The Analog-to-Digital Converter

The physical axon is not the sensor. The transmission cable is wrapped in Schwann cell insulation and contains voltage-gated channels designed solely to repeat identical electrical waves. Sensing occurs exclusively at the free nerve endings (terminal arborizations).

Embedded across the lipid bilayer of these terminal endings sits an array of specialized transmembrane receptor&nbsp;proteins:

* **ASIC1 and ASIC3 (Acid-Sensing Ion Channels):** Proton-gated ion channels displaying 5x to 10x higher density in oral sensory fibers than in somatic skin. Free hydrogen ions (H+) from dietary acids or bacterial lactic acid bind directly to their external vestibules.
* **TRPV1:** Non-selective cation channels that open in response to noxious heat or capsaicin.
* **TRPA1:** Reactive irritant and noxious cold sensors.

### The Mechanical Signaling Cascade
1. **Ligand Docking:** Free protons (H+) bind to the outer loops of ASIC3 channels, twisting the central pore open.
2. **Analog Ingress:** Extracellular sodium (Na+) and calcium (Ca2+) rush inward, acting as an analog generator current (I_stim).
3. **Capacitive Charging:** The terminal membrane functions as a capacitor (C_m). The influx of positive ions pushes the local membrane potential (V_m) upward from -70 mV toward -55 mV.
4. **Digital Ignition (Nav1.7):** At the unmyelinated trigger zone where terminal branches merge, clustered Nav1.7 voltage-gated sodium channels act as an electronic comparator. The moment voltage crosses -55 mV, they snap open, converting the pooled analog charge into an all-or-none, discrete binary pulse (an action&nbsp;potential).

Because an action potential is binary, an individual wave cannot encode severity by changing its amplitude. The system communicates stimulus intensity entirely via **Pulse-Frequency Modulation (PFM)**: firing more pulses per second down the line.

## Trunk Bandwidth: The Mathematical Limit of Cable Capacity

Once converted into digital pulses, the signals travel through the main branches of the Trigeminal Nerve (CN V): the Maxillary nerve (V2) for the upper arch and palate, and the Mandibular nerve (V3) for the lower jaw and&nbsp;tongue.

The data carrying capacity of this physical cable is governed by the Absolute Refractory Period (t_ref), which is the 1 to 2 milliseconds during which Nav1.7 channels remain inactivated and cannot conduct another wave:

```
F_max = 1 / t_ref
```

* **Unmyelinated C-Fibers (Burning, Scalding Sensations):** Sustained firing ceilings sit at 20 to 50 Hz.
* **Thinly Myelinated A-delta Fibers (Sharp, Stinging Sensations):** Burst limits reach 100 to 300 Hz.

```
[ SINGLE C-FIBER CLOCK RATE ]
Quiet Baseline:        ─── | ────── | ────── | ────── | ───  (< 1 Hz)
Moderate Irritation:   ── | ── | ── | ── | ── | ── | ── | ──  (10 - 15 Hz)
Refractory Ceiling:    ─||||||||||||||||||||||||||||||||||||─  (30 - 50 Hz: Cable Maxed)
```

When considering the entire nerve trunk, a branch like V3 carries roughly 15,000 to 20,000 small nociceptive fibers. Between 70% and 80% of these fibers express functional ASIC proton channels.

Under normal, buffered conditions (salivary pH 7.0 to 7.4), small fibers fire occasional maintenance pulses (less than 1 Hz), consuming less than 2% of the cable's carrying capacity. 

However, when dental cement washouts or root recession expose dentinal tubules, subgingival bacterial fermentation drops local pH below 6.2. ASIC3 channels open and remain locked open due to their non-inactivating sustained currents.

If just 2,000 unarmored fibers along an unsealed tooth margin are driven to 50 Hz, that single site pumps **100,000 action potentials every second into the mandibular canal**. The localized cable hits full saturation without requiring a visible hole or large structural cavity.

## The Central Switchboard: Brainstem Buffer Overflow

All primary sensory fibers from V1 (eyes/forehead), V2 (upper jaw/palate), and V3 (lower jaw/tongue) terminate in a shared brainstem processing exchange: the **Subnucleus Caudalis** of the Spinal Trigeminal&nbsp;Nucleus.

At this central relay, the physical axon ends. The electrical wave cannot jump across the open 20-nanometer synaptic cleft. To cross the divide, the arrival of each action potential forces local voltage-gated calcium channels open, prompting synaptic vesicles to fuse with the presynaptic membrane and release discrete packets of neurotransmitters:

* **Glutamate:** The high-speed primary pain neurotransmitter.
* **CGRP and Substance P:** Slow-acting neuropeptides that amplify excitability and drive neurogenic inflammation.

```
Incoming Spikes (λ)                Synaptic Cleft                     Astrocytic Clearance (μ)
──────────────────────▶ [ Vesicles Dump Glutamate / CGRP ] ────────▶ [ Glial Vacuum Thread ]
                                        │
                                        │ (When λ > μ: Buffer Overflow)
                                        ▼
                         [ Volume Transmission / Spillover ]
                                        │
                      ┌─────────────────┴─────────────────┐
                      ▼                                   ▼
          [ Palatine Line (V2) ]                 [ Nasal Line (V1) ]
           "Scalded Palate"                       "Burning Nose"
```

Under low-bandwidth conditions, surrounding glial cells (astrocytes) act like an active cleanup thread, vacuuming up glutamate within milliseconds to ensure signals remain isolated to their specific pathway.

The system obeys the principles of queueing theory. When incoming packet arrival ($\lambda$) exceeds astrocytic clearance capacity ($\mu$):

1. **Clearance Saturation:** Astrocytes cannot reabsorb the glutamate deluge fast enough.
2. **Magnesium Unblock (NMDA Activation):** Sustained depolarization expels the protective magnesium ion (Mg2+) blocking postsynaptic NMDA receptors, locking the second-order neurons into a persistent high-gain state (central wind-up).
3. **Volume Transmission:** Excess neurotransmitters physically spill outside the primary synaptic cleft and drift through the extracellular fluid of the nucleus.
4. **Cross-Port Bleed:** Escaped glutamate and CGRP depolarize adjacent second-order dendrites dedicated to the palate (V2), the nasal passages (V1), and the ear canal (V3 via the auriculotemporal nerve).

## Cortical DNS Resolution: The Whole-Face Alarm

From the Subnucleus Caudalis, second-order projection fibers ascend via the quintothalamic tract to the **Ventral Posteromedial (VPM)** nucleus of the thalamus, which routes the signals directly to the **Primary Somatosensory Cortex (S1)**.

The cortex possesses a hardwired topological map of the body: the sensory homunculus. The brain relies on physical cabling proximity to identify signal&nbsp;sources:

* When line V2 fires into the thalamus, the brain computes: *Scalded hard palate.*
* When line V1 fires into the thalamus, the brain computes: *Hot, dry nasal passages.*
* When auriculotemporal V3 fires into the thalamus, the brain computes: *Deep radiating ear canal pain.*

The conscious brain is not imagining symptoms, nor is it suffering from an unprovoked psychological delusion. It is faithfully decoding the multi-channel flood arriving at its sensory cortex. 

It cannot differentiate between a direct scald on the roof of the mouth and a neurotransmitter spillover triggered by an unsealed tooth margin downstream.

## Resolving the Circuit

Labeling persistent oral burning as "idiopathic" occurs when clinical diagnostic tools lack the resolution to detect component-level hardware faults. A 100-micron metal explorer pick sweeps smoothly across a 1-micron microleakage gap, and 2D bitewings cannot peer through radiopaque porcelain or metal crowns to identify sub-micron cement washouts.

The trigeminal pathway is an analog-to-digital network governed by fluid dynamics, membrane capacitance, and central clearing limits. Prescribing central neuromodulators to manage burning mouth symptoms simply sedates the central switchboard while leaving peripheral sensors submerged in an active chemical bath.

Restoring long-term stability requires treating the mouth as an engineering circuit: finding the micro-structural entry points, blocking open tubules, and eliminating the acid fuels that keep the peripheral sensors clamped open. 

When the peripheral inputs are sealed, the primary cables drop back below their refractory ceilings, the brainstem buffer clears, and the central alarm goes&nbsp;silent.
