## 1. The Hydrodynamic Vacuum Chamber

Underneath your hard outer enamel sits **dentin**, a porous living tissue honeycombed with tens of thousands of microscopic channels called **dentinal tubules**. Inside each tubule is dentinal fluid, in direct communication with the pulp nerve fibers.

When a dentist cleans out decay and places a composite filling, resin shrinkage or improper adaptation can leave behind an invisible **micro-void or air pocket** between the composite and the tooth floor.

### What happens when you eat?
That tiny air gap behaves as a **vacuum pressure chamber**:
1. When you bite down on food or sip hot, cold, or acidic liquids, the temperature and pressure in that tiny void fluctuate wildly.
2. The pressure differential causes the dentinal fluid inside the tubules to pump violently in and out (the Hydrodynamic Mechanism of Brännström).
3. The movement of that fluid stretches and fires the mechanoreceptors and nociceptors at the pulp border, sending sharp shockwaves straight into the trigeminal nerve.

If you already suffer from sensitized trigeminal pathways (as in Burning Mouth Syndrome), that continuous micro-pumping acts like fuel on an open flame.

---

## 2. The Solution: Immediate Dentin Sealing (IDS)

In 2005, Dr. Pascal Magne published a landmark clinical protocol that fundamentally changed biomimetic dentistry: **Immediate Dentin Sealing (IDS)**.

Instead of cutting a tooth and placing composite directly over raw, open dentin, the dentist immediately seals the dentinal tubules *at the preparation stage*, before impressions or final restorations are placed.

### Why IDS Changes the Entire Game
If the dentin is sealed immediately:
* The dentinal tubules are blocked at their openings.
* Even if a microscopic air void exists beneath the subsequent filling, **fluid movement cannot happen** because there is no open conduit for pressure changes to reach the nerve.
* The patient perceives zero post-operative hydrodynamic pain.

## 3. The Clinical Protocol: How Top Dentists Do It

The specialist highlights the updated 2021 consensus protocol by Dr. Pascal Magne and Dr. Marco Gresnigt:

1. **High Magnification (Loupes or Dental Microscope):**  
   The dentist must visually distinguish enamel margins from raw dentin. You cannot seal what you cannot clearly resolve under light.
2. **Selective Etching:**  
   Enamel is etched first for 20 seconds, followed by dentin for 20 seconds, then thoroughly rinsed and gently dried without desiccating the collagen matrix.
3. **Filled Adhesive (OptiBond FL or Equivalent):**  
   A filled, high-strength adhesive is meticulously applied to the dentin surface.
4. **The Flowable Composite Resin Coating (RBC):**  
   In regions where OptiBond FL is unavailable or to boost bond strength, top clinicians immediately apply a micro-thin layer of flowable composite over the cured adhesive on the dentin only. This seals the hybrid layer, elevates bond strength, and creates a hermetic shield.
5. **Eliminating the Oxygen-Inhibition Layer:**  
   Because ambient oxygen leaves an uncured monomer residue on the surface, this sticky layer must be removed (often with an alcohol wipe or air-blocking barrier) so it does not interfere with final impressions or bonding.

## 4. What This Means for Burning Mouth & Orofacial Pain Patients

Many patients diagnosed with "idiopathic" burning mouth can trace their initial symptoms to a dental intervention: a deep filling, a crown prep, or an old composite replacement.

If that restoration was completed with delayed dentin sealing, unsealed margins, or trapped micro-voids, the tooth acts as a silent, continuous trigeminal irritant. The patient doesn't always feel sharp tooth pain; instead, the constant low-grade sensory bombardment spills over into the lingual and mental nerve branches, manifesting as burning tongue, cheek tenderness, or ear fullness.

### Questions to Ask Your Restorative Dentist:
If you are planning fillings, onlays, or crown work:
- *"Do you use Immediate Dentin Sealing (IDS) on the exposed dentin before taking impressions or layering composite?"*
- *"Do you use selective etch and a filled bonding system like OptiBond FL with a flowable resin coating?"*
- *"Do you work under high-power dental loops or an operating microscope to verify margin seals?"*

When dental hardware is sealed at the microscopic tubule level, you eliminate the mechanical vacuum chambers that keep the trigeminal circuit in chronic distress.
