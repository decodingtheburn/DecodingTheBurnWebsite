## 1. The Human vs Rodent Trap: 100% of Human Pain Neurons Sense Acid

For decades, chronic pain research relied heavily on rodent models. In rats, electrophysiological testing shows that only about **30%** of dorsal root ganglion (DRG) sensory neurons respond to acidic stimuli. 

When Dr. Nien-Du Yang and the Latigo team conducted an automated patch-clamp census on **human** sensory neurons, the species difference was&nbsp;staggering:

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|              SENSORY NEURON ACID SENSITIVITY: RAT VS HUMAN                      |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                                                |
|   RAT DRG NEURONS:                                                             |
|   [ 30% ASIC Responsive ] [ 70% Non-Responsive ]                              |
|                                                                                |
|   HUMAN DRG NEURONS:                                                           |
|   [========================= 100% ASIC RESPONSIVE =========================]   |
|                                                                                |
|   Every single human sensory neuron tested expressed active ASIC currents.     |
|   Every single human sensory neuron responded to ASIC3 modulation.             |
|                                                                                |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Every single human sensory neuron tested was positive for ASIC currents, with **ASIC1 and ASIC3** forming the predominant channel subunits. 

Furthermore, RNAscope imaging proved that ASIC1 and ASIC3 expression directly overlaps with **SCN10A (NaV1.8)**, the definitive genetic biomarker for peripheral nociceptors (pain-sensing nerves). 

Human beings are wired with an exquisite, ubiquitous peripheral alarm system dedicated strictly to detecting extracellular&nbsp;protons.

## 2. Mild Acidification Triggers the "Currency of Pain"

A common objection from clinicians is: *"Saliva buffers acid quickly, and food passes in seconds. How could acid cause round-the-clock burning?"*

The patch-clamp traces presented by Latigo provide the answer. Sensory neurons do not require caustic battery acid or violent gastric reflux (pH 1.5) to activate. 

When human sensory neurons were exposed to mild, physiological drops in pH (from neutral down to **pH 7.0 and pH 6.3**):

1. **Immediate Inward Sodium Currents:** The extracellular acidic pocket of the ASIC trimer undergoes an immediate conformational shift. The transmembrane pore snaps open, allowing sodium (Na<sup>+</sup>) ions to flood into the nerve terminal.
2. **Direct Action Potential Firing:** This rapid influx depolarizes the neuronal cell membrane, directly evoking action potentials, what Dr. Yang described as the fundamental *"currency of pain"*.
3. **Heightened Excitability:** Even when acidification was too subtle to evoke a full action potential on its own, it maintained the membrane in a partially depolarized state, lowering the threshold so that minor mechanical pressure or warmth caused the nerve to fire violently.

```
Dietary / Bacterial Acid (pH 6.3 to 7.0)
                 │
                 ▼
    Binds Extracellular Pocket of ASIC3 / ASIC1
                 │
                 ▼
    Pore Opens: Sodium (Na+) Floods Sensory Nerve
                 │
                 ▼
    Membrane Depolarization (Action Potentials Evoked)
                 │
                 ▼
    Trigeminal Pain Circuit: Burning Sensation Perceived
```

## 3. What Is Eurofins DiscoverX and Why Are They Doing This?

For context, **Eurofins DiscoverX** is not a dental company. They are the drug-discovery products branch of Eurofins Scientific, specializing in automated patch-clamp screening platforms (such as the QPatch and SynchroPatch). 

Pharmaceutical companies partner with Eurofins DiscoverX to test drug candidates against cloned ion channels and human cell lines. 

**Latigo Biotherapeutics** is a clinical-stage biotech developing non-opioid pain medications. To find molecules that can block acid-induced pain without the side effects of opioids or central antidepressants, researchers needed a pharmacological tool to manipulate ASIC3 with extreme precision. 

They synthesized **WRPRFa**, an engineered peptide based on a compound found in cone snail venom (Rfamide). By slowing the closing (desensitization) of ASIC3 channels, WRPRFa locks the channel in an open state, allowing researchers to study how acid blockers (like amiloride or novel small molecules) attach to the&nbsp;pore.

The fact that venture-backed biopharma companies are investing millions of dollars to develop selective ASIC3 modulators confirms what BMS patients experience daily: **acid-sensing ion channels on peripheral sensory nerves are powerful, independent drivers of chronic pain**.

## 5. Bridging the Gap: What This Means for Patients

Understanding that human pain neurons possess high ASIC3 sensitivity changes how we approach recovery:

1. **Eliminate the Fuel (Dietary Acid):** Lowering dietary acid intake (avoiding citric acid, vinegars, coffee, fermented sauces, and carbohydrate-driven oral bacterial fermentation) removes the trigger that snaps ASIC channels open.
2. **Seal the Hardware Breaches:** Using proven dental barrier protocols, like **Gluma** to precipitate albumin plugs inside open dentin tubules, or **Tokuyama Shield Force Plus** to armor enamel craze lines, physically blocks protons from reaching the receptors.
3. **Validate Your Symptoms:** If a healthcare provider suggests that your burning is imaginary because your oral tissues look normal, remember the electrophysiology: **100% of human sensory pain neurons fire when extracellular pH drops**. 

Pain does not require an open wound. It only requires an open ion&nbsp;channel.
