Most routine dental exams follow a standard pattern: the dental assistant takes new bitewing radiographs, the dentist sits down, looks at the fresh images on screen for 60 seconds to check for active decay or visible abscesses, and says: *"Looks good, no cavities!"*

While this works well for routine cavity checks, it misses progressive, slow-moving structural changes that trigger chronic nerve pain and Burning Mouth Syndrome (BMS).

To catch gradual bone height loss, crestal lamina dura thinning, and root exposure, you need **Time-Series Radiographic Comparison**—comparing your newest x-ray side-by-side against images taken 3, 5, or 10 years ago.

---

## 1. Why Single-Image X-Ray Checks Miss BMS Triggers

When a clinician only evaluates your latest radiograph:
- **Static vs. Dynamic:** They see a single static snapshot in time. If your alveolar bone level is 2mm below the cementoenamel junction (CEJ), it looks "within normal limits" on a single image.
- **The Progression Hidden in Plain Sight:** If that same bone line was 0.5mm below the CEJ five years ago, you have lost 1.5mm of supporting bone height around that tooth. That 1.5mm drop is where dentinal tubules become exposed subgingivally, creating unsealed pathways for bacterial and dietary acid to reach pulp nerves.

Without comparing older images side-by-side, **progressive loss is invisible**.

```
Single View (Static):     [2mm Bone Loss] ──► "Looks normal for your age."
Time-Series (Dynamic):   [0.5mm ──► 2.0mm] ──► "Active 1.5mm recession & crestal loss!"
```

---

## 2. What to Ask When Booking an Appointment or Interviewing a New Dentist

When calling to book a consultation or evaluation with a new dentist, ask this exact question upfront:

> **"When you evaluate x-rays during an exam, do you just review the latest set taken today, or do you overlay and compare them side-by-side with older historic x-rays from previous years to check for progressive bone loss and root changes?"**

### How to Evaluate Their Response:

| Reception / Dentist Answer | What It Tells You |
|---|---|
| 🚩 *"We usually just take new ones every 12-18 months to check for current cavities."* | Standard reactive practice. They are looking for acute decay, not subtle time-series progression. |
| 🟡 *"We can look at old ones if you bring them, but the doctor mostly looks at today's images."* | Passive approach. You will need to explicitly push them to compare during the visit. |
| 💚 *"We encourage bringing prior digital records! Dr. [Name] always opens historic DICOM files side-by-side to track changes over time."* | **Ideal provider.** This clinician understands longitudinal structural changes. |

## 3. How to Request a Time-Series Comparison During Your Visit

If you are already in the dental chair:

1. **Provide Your Historic Files:** Hand the front desk or dental assistant your USB drive or email link containing your historic DICOM/JPEG x-rays (from 3–5+ years ago) obtained using [The Dental Records Access Playbook](/blog/how-to-request-your-dental-records-xrays-perio-charts-and-notes/).
2. **Request Side-by-Side Display:**
   > *"I brought my digital bitewings from 2021 and 2023. Could we pull them up side-by-side with today's images on the monitor? I'd like to look specifically at the bone crest height around tooth #[Number] where I experience persistent burning."*
3. **What to Look For Together:**
   - **Interdental Crest Height:** Has the thin white line of bone between the teeth dropped lower relative to the enamel line?
   - **Root Margin Junction:** Is more of the darker, porous root dentin visible now than 3 years ago?
   - **Restoration Margins:** Has the bone or gum line receded below the edge of an old filling or crown margin?
