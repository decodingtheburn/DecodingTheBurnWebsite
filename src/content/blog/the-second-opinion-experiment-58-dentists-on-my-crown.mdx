When dealing with chronic oral burning, hidden dental leaks, or aging restorations, getting a single dentist's opinion is rarely enough.

A few months ago, I posted a real X-ray of a 20-year-old crown (Tooth #36) to an online group of practicing dentists, prosthodontists, and dental hygienists. I asked a simple, open question:

> *"I have a crown that has this 'imperfection'. What would you do if you saw this X-ray in your clinic?"*

What followed was an eye-opening diagnostic experiment that every dental patient needs to see.

---

## The Tally: 84.5% Voted REDO vs. 15.5% Conservative Watch

Out of 58 detailed responses from dental professionals, the vast majority identified a severe **open margin** and warned that keeping the crown was a "ticking time bomb."

## The Outcome: 3 Hidden Cavities Underneath

While a minority of conservative dentists suggested: *"If it's been there 20 years and doesn't hurt, don't touch it,"* the physical reality proved otherwise.

When my new dentist finally removed the 20-year-old crown, **there were 3 active cavities festering underneath the porcelain margin.**

### The Danger of Short-Term "Conservative" Comfort:
* **Short-Term Savings vs. Long-Term Disaster:** A conservative "watch and wait" approach might save money in the short term, but letting trapped acid and bacteria pool under an open margin for 20 years can destroy the underlying tooth structure.
* **Risk of Total Tooth Loss:** If I had let it go for another few years based on the "don't touch it if it doesn't hurt" advice, the decay would have penetrated the root pulp, forcing an emergency root canal or a full extraction.
* **The BMS Connection:** Trapped bacterial acid under open margins creates a 24/7 localized proton reservoir directly against exposed dentinal tubules, constantly hyper-sensitizing peripheral trigeminal nerves.

## How to Get Free Second Opinions from Online Dentists

You do not have to rely on a single opinion when evaluating suspicious crowns, open margins, or old root canals. 

By taking high-resolution photos of your dental radiographs or CBCT scans and posting them to public professional groups like [Ask a Dental Expert!](https://www.facebook.com/groups/1306418723079290/), you can get **objective, crowdsourced feedback from dozens of independent dentists for free**.

## Key Takeaway for Patients

Never confuse "no immediate pain" with "no active decay." Aging dental work with open margins will eventually fail. Crowdsourcing second opinions from independent clinicians gives you the data you need to take control of your dental health before temporary leaks become permanent losses.
