## 1. Why Test Avocado? The Biochemical&nbsp;Rationale

Most fruits are absolute minefields for Burning Mouth Syndrome. Oranges, pineapples, lemons, and strawberries carry heavy organic acid loads (citric, malic, and ascorbic acids) that bring oral pH down into the 2.5 to 3.8 range, instantly opening Acid-Sensing Ion Channels (ASICs). Other fruits like apples, grapes, and bananas carry high concentrations of fructose and sucrose, which feed subgingival biofilms and produce localized lactic acid within hours of&nbsp;eating.

Avocado (*Persea americana*), however, represents a fascinating biological exception in the plant&nbsp;kingdom:

1. **Near-Neutral Acidity (pH 6.2 to 6.6):** Unlike acidic citrus or tomatoes, fresh avocado does not bathe oral tissues in concentrated hydrogen protons (H<sup>+</sup>). Direct contact with the tongue produces zero acute acidic sting or barrier&nbsp;shock.
2. **High Monounsaturated Fat Content:** Over 75% of avocado calories come from oleic acid and healthy fats, which typically slow gastric emptying and create a soothing, non-corrosive oral coat.
3. **The Hidden Variable (Fermentable Fibers & Polyols):** While avocado contains less than 1 gram of sugar per fruit, it does contain roughly 7 grams of dietary fiber (including soluble polysaccharides) and small amounts of persitol/polyols.

The core question of this experiment is straightforward: **Does avocado fiber and cellular pulp lodge in gingival sulci or restoration micro-margins and ferment into pain-triggering acids over 48 to 72 hours, or does its low acidity keep trigeminal nociceptors completely silent?**
