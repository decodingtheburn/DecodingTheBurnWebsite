Yes, an acid leak can create inflammation, but **it can also cause pure, high-voltage burning pain without creating visible clinical&nbsp;inflammation**.

This distinction explains why patients get trapped in the "mystery" of Burning Mouth Syndrome: clinicians look for red, swollen tissue, see none, and dismiss the pain as&nbsp;psychological.

The physical and biological dynamics break down across these two&nbsp;scenarios:

---

### Scenario A: When Acid DOES Create&nbsp;Inflammation

When acid leaks into living vascular tissue, it triggers inflammation through two distinct&nbsp;mechanisms:

1. **Neurogenic Inflammation (Inside the Tooth Pulp):**
   * When hydrogen protons (H⁺) seep through exposed dentinal tubules or under a leaking crown margin, they bind to **Acid-Sensing Ion Channels (ASIC1 and ASIC3)** on intradental pulpal&nbsp;nerves.
   * Via the *axon reflex*, these activated nerve endings release powerful vasoactive neuropeptides directly into the pulp: **Calcitonin Gene-Related Peptide (CGRP)** and **Substance&nbsp;P**.
   * CGRP causes immediate micro-arteriole dilation, while Substance P increases capillary permeability, leaking fluid into the extracellular space. Because the dental pulp is enclosed in rigid, unyielding dentin walls, this fluid leakage spikes **intrapulpal tissue pressure** (pulpal inflammation /&nbsp;pulpitis).

2. **Subgingival Chemical Irritation (In the Gum Pocket):**
   * If lactic acid pools inside a 3 to 4 mm gum pocket, it acts as a caustic chemical agent on the thin, non-keratinized sulcular&nbsp;lining.
   * It activates host **matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)** that break down collagen, impairs tissue repair, and causes micro-capillary leakage (bleeding on&nbsp;probing).

---

### Scenario B: When Acid DOES NOT Create Visible&nbsp;Inflammation

Acid can generate a 10/10 burning firestorm while the mouth looks completely calm and&nbsp;pink:

1. **Direct Electrochemical Gating (The "Pure Telemetry" Pathway):**
   * Proton-sensing ion channels (ASICs) and vanilloid receptors (TRPV1) do not require immune cells, histamine, or swelling to&nbsp;fire.
   * When hydrogen ions (H⁺) touch an unshielded sensory nerve terminal, they function purely as a chemical key in an electrical lock: the channel snaps open, sodium and calcium rush in, and a train of action potentials shoots straight to the&nbsp;brainstem.
   * The sensation of burning is instantaneous, but because no macroscopic cellular destruction or immune recruitment has occurred, there is zero swelling, zero erythema (redness), and zero&nbsp;blistering.

2. **Microscopic Smear Plug Dissolution:**
   * At pH 6.2 to 6.7, acid chemically dissolves the crystalline mineral plugs capping exposed root tubules without causing any soft-tissue&nbsp;trauma.
   * This unseals fluid movement (hydrodynamic shear), pulling on mechanoreceptors and burning ASIC gates without leaving any visual trace on standard dental mirrors or 2D&nbsp;X-rays.

3. **Brainstem Gain and Referred Burning:**
   * When a microscopic leak sends relentless signals into the brainstem’s **Subnucleus Caudalis**, the central switchboard&nbsp;overflows.
   * Neurotransmitter spillover (glutamate, CGRP) bleeds across adjacent branches, making the cortex perceive that the entire tongue, roof of the mouth, or lips are scalded raw. When a doctor looks at that burning tongue, the mucosa is completely healthy and uninflamed because the tongue is only the "speaker" projecting the alarm: the actual leak is at a tooth margin across the&nbsp;arch.

---

### Summary

* **Can an acid leak create inflammation?** Yes, via **neurogenic peptide dumping (CGRP/Substance P)** in the pulp chamber and chemical irritation of the sulcular&nbsp;crevice.
* **Can it trigger pain without inflammation?** Yes, because **free protons directly depolarize sensory nerve gates (ASICs)**, sending high-voltage burning signals to the brain while the tissues appear entirely normal and&nbsp;pink.
