If you have Burning Mouth Syndrome (BMS), you have likely sat in a dentist's chair, explained that your mouth feels scalded, and listened to the same frustrating response:

> *"Your gums look healthy, there are no cavities on your bitewing X-rays, and your oral tissue looks completely pink and normal. There is nothing dental causing your burning."*

This clinical breakdown happens because routine dental exams are designed to look for active bacterial decay (caries) and gross gum disease (periodontitis). They are **not** routinely calibrated to quantify gradual chemical erosion, cupped-out occlusal enamel, or exposed dentinal root collars.

To bridge this gap and uncover the physical hardware breaches driving your symptoms, you need an objective, standardized metric. 

The gold standard in dental literature is the **Basic Erosive Wear Examination (BEWE)**.

---

## What Is a BEWE (Basic Erosive Wear Examination)?

Developed in 2008 by leading international dental researchers (Prof. David Bartlett, Prof. Adrian Lussi, and Dr. Carolina Ganss), the BEWE is a validated, standardized screening tool that quantifies the severity of **erosive tooth wear (ETW)** across your entire mouth.

Instead of vague statements like *"you have a bit of enamel wear,"* the BEWE gives you and your clinician an objective, reproducible score from **0 to 18**.

### How the Mouth Is Divided (6 Sextants)
Your dental arches are divided into six functional zones:
- **Sextant 1:** Upper right molars and premolars
- **Sextant 2:** Upper front teeth (incisors and canines)
- **Sextant 3:** Upper left molars and premolars
- **Sextant 4:** Lower left molars and premolars
- **Sextant 5:** Lower front teeth (incisors and canines)
- **Sextant 6:** Lower right molars and premolars

## Cumulative Score & Clinical Risk Levels

The worst scores from all six sextants are summed to produce your **Cumulative BEWE Score (0 to 18)**:

| Cumulative Score | Clinical Risk Level | Official Management Recommendation |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **0 – 2** | **Minimal / None** | Routine observation, baseline monitoring. |
| **3 – 8** | **Low Risk** | Dietary acid review, oral hygiene audit, fluorides. |
| **9 – 13** | **Medium Risk** | Active identification of acid triggers (dietary, GERD, medications), intensive desensitization, close 6-month monitoring. |
| **14 – 18** | **High Risk** | **Restorative intervention indicated:** Bonded composite resin shields, crown replacement, and occlusal rehabilitation. |

---

## Why the BEWE Is a Game-Changer for BMS Patients

In a typical BMS patient who has consumed coffee, sodas, citrus, or wine over 20 to 40 years, the BEWE almost always reveals a **Cumulative Score of 9 to 15+ (Medium to High Risk)**.

Here is why this score unlocks clinical breakthroughs:

1. **It Proves the Loss of the Enamel Seal:** A score of 2 or 3 in any sextant means **dentin is exposed**. Living dentin has 20,000 to 45,000 open microscopic tubules per mm<sup>2</sup> leading directly to pulpal nerve fibers.
2. **It Validates the Acid Connection:** When you present your BEWE score alongside your dietary record, the dentist can no longer dismiss your burning mouth as "idiopathic." The biophysical mechanism becomes obvious: ambient acids are leaking through breached enamel and stimulating Acid-Sensing Ion Channels (ASIC3).
3. **It Justifies Restorative Sealing:** Insurance and dental protocols often hesitate to treat wear unless it is categorized as pathological. A high BEWE score provides objective documentation that justifies placing cervical composite patches or replacing compromised crowns.

## Word-for-Word Scripts: How to Request a BEWE

You do not need to be confrontational. Use these professional, collaborative scripts at your next dental visit:

### When Booking the Appointment:
> *"Hi, I am booking my next exam, and I would specifically like to request a Basic Erosive Wear Examination (BEWE) to score and document erosive tooth wear across my six sextants. Could you note that in my chart for the dentist?"*

### Chairside With the Dentist:
> *"Doctor, I am managing chronic oral neuropathic burning. I know my oral mucosa looks visually healthy, but our community research strongly links my symptoms to exposed dentinal tubules and chemical acid ingress.*
> 
> *Could we perform a quick BEWE scoring across my six sextants today? I want to record my baseline cumulative score so we can see whether my enamel loss has entered the medium-to-high risk category, particularly around my premolar cusps, palatal incisors, and receded cervical margins."*

### If the Dentist Is Unfamiliar With the Acronym:
> *"It is the Basic Erosive Wear Examination established by Bartlett and Lussi in 2008. It just takes two minutes: you score the single worst surface in each of the six sextants from 0 to 3 based on whether enamel is intact, whether dentin is exposed under 50%, or over 50%. Then we add them up out of 18."*

## Action Plan After Getting Your Score

Once your dentist calculates your cumulative BEWE score, align your next steps based on your score tier:

1. **If Score is 9 to 13 (Medium Risk):**
   - Request in-office **Gluma desensitizer** (glutaraldehyde/HEMA) over all sensitive or receded root collars to coagulate tubule fluid and test symptom response.
   - Switch to a 0.454% stannous fluoride or nano-hydroxyapatite daily regimen.
   - Implement a strict zero-acid dietary baseline.
2. **If Score is 14 to 18 (High Risk):**
   - Schedule appointments to physically seal the breached tooth structure using **biomimetic cervical composite patches**.
   - Audit all existing dental crowns for cement washout and micro-leakage using tactile explorer checks and isolated air tests.
   - Take intra-oral 3D scans to track vertical dimension loss over time.
